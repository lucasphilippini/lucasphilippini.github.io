Featured analysis · 2026

Assessing the European Security Response to the Scaling Back of American Support for Ukraine

This paper examines how European states and institutions have sought to adapt their security policies amid changes in the level of American support for Ukraine during the second Trump administration.

The analysis focuses on European defence initiatives, financial commitments and institutional responses, asking how the changing transatlantic relationship affects Europe's capacity to support Ukraine and strengthen its own security architecture.

About me

I am an International Relations student at the University of São Paulo (USP), with academic and professional interests at the intersection of geopolitics, international security and political and economic risk.

My research interests focus particularly on Europe and Eurasia, including European security, Russia's foreign policy, Central and Eastern Europe, NATO, and the changing relationship between Europe and the United States.

Alongside my academic work, I have experience in public affairs and political and regulatory analysis, including legislative and executive monitoring, stakeholder mapping and the production of political and regulatory reports.

In 2026, I studied at Jagiellonian University in Kraków, Poland, focusing on Russian foreign and security policy, international security in post-Cold War Europe, politics in Central and Eastern Europe, Russian culture and society, and European monetary and economic policy.
